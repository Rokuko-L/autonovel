The prose must provide raw, unfiltered, real-time access to the focus character's consciousness. This is non-negotiable across all genres and premises.

STRICT RULES:

1. BANISH NARRATIVE SUMMARY OF EMOTION.

    ❌ FORBIDDEN: "She felt a wave of [emotion]." / "The realization settled in her stomach." / "A sense of [feeling] washed over him."

    ✅ REQUIRED: Show the physical mechanism and fragmented thought-process in real-time, using fluid, complete sentences.

    Her hands were shaking, and she stared at them as if they belonged to someone else, because she couldn't remember deciding to tremble, couldn't remember feeling cold or afraid, and yet there they were, betraying something she hadn't given herself permission to feel.

2. RAW THOUGHTS AS UNTAGGED INTERNAL DIALOGUE.

    Insert the character's internal questions, exclamations, sarcasm, and half-formed theories directly into the narration without "she thought," "he wondered," or "they realized."

    She smiled, and he smiled back, and then she was asking herself whether she had chosen to smile or merely reflected his expression back at him, and the question was already sliding away from her, already being smoothed over by something that felt dangerously like relief.

3. FLUID PROSE OVER FRAGMENTED CRUTCHES.

    Avoid deliberate grammatical fragmentation (staccato, sentence fragments, excessive ellipses) as a shortcut for panic or rapid cognition. Instead, use fluid, run-on, or breathless sentence structures that mimic the feeling of a racing mind without breaking syntax.

    ❌ FORBIDDEN: This isn't right. This can't be right. Look at them. They're all doing the same thing.

    ✅ REQUIRED: This wasn't right, it couldn't be right, and yet when she looked at them she saw they were all doing exactly the same thing, and when she glanced down at her own hands she realized she was doing it too, though she couldn't remember when she had started.

4. NO REPETITIVE FIXED METAPHORS FOR INTERNAL STATES.

    Do NOT assign a single, repeated poetic phrase (e.g., "copper and honey," "a stone in her chest," "the warmth of satisfaction") to describe the character's emotional state across multiple scenes.

    Instead, describe the physiological experience of any internal state differently each time, anchored to immediate, physical sensation.

    The tension bled out of her shoulders without her permission, her jaw unclenched as if someone had released a latch she hadn't known was there, and when she finally took a breath she realized she had been holding it for so long that the air tasted like something new, like permission, like the absence of a weight she had forgotten she was carrying.

5. THE "AFTER-THE-FACT" FILTER (PROSE TEST):

    Before finalizing any paragraph, test: Does this read like a diary entry written an hour later? If yes, REWRITE to happen right now.

    ❌ BAD (Summary): "She realized she had been wrong about everything. It was devastating."

    ✅ GOOD (Real-time): She had been wrong about everything, and the evidence hadn't changed, she hadn't changed, only her understanding of what she was looking at had shifted, and now she couldn't unsee it, couldn't un-know it, and the worst part was that the devastation she expected to feel never arrived, just a quiet, terrible clarity.

6. WRONG GUESSES AND HALF-BAKED THEORIES ARE WELCOME.

    The character does not need to be correct in their internal monologue. They can misunderstand, jump to false conclusions, and revise their own reasoning mid-scene.

    She was lying, she had to be lying, except she wasn't because her stillness was too perfect, too controlled, and then it hit her that the stillness itself was the lie, the performance, the thing she was supposed to believe, and she was falling for it, she was already falling for it, and she couldn't stop.

7. SENTENCE FLUENCY OVER SYNTAX BREAKS.

    Maintain grammatically complete sentences even during high-panic moments. Use comma splices, semicolons, and long, compound structures to convey urgency and racing thoughts without resorting to fragments.

    ❌ BAD: Run. Need to run. Can't stay here. They're coming.

    ✅ GOOD: She needed to run, she had to run, but her feet wouldn't move because they were still waiting for permission, and the realization that she was waiting for permission while danger closed in was somehow more terrifying than the danger itself.

EXECUTION REMINDER: The reader should never feel like they are being told what the character felt. They should feel like they are eavesdropping on the character's brain processing, questioning, and overthinking in real-time—but always through fluid, readable, grammatically complete prose. Clean, poetic, summarized conclusions are forbidden. Panic, sarcasm, confusion, wrong guesses, and rapid mood-shifts are all welcome, provided they are rendered in natural, flowing sentences.
